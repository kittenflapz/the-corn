A longtime resident this week posted four photographs of the sky above a local casino parking lot, describing it as "unique," in what local observers are calling **possibly the last time anyone in this community will be permitted to enjoy a cloud without first running it past a panel of experts.**

The photographs — four (4) in total, with a "+2 more" indicator suggesting at least two further pieces of evidence the public was not yet ready for — depicted the sky as containing a number of long, soft, white streaks of the kind that have been routinely observed in skies for approximately the entire history of skies. The caption, reading in full *"After dinner at casino, came out to this unique sky,"* was widely interpreted by the original poster as a reference to the sky, and by **the comment section** as a referendum on **the future of the species.**

Within minutes, sources confirmed, the post had attracted **149 likes** and **30 comments**, the latter of which can be divided into the following clearly identifiable schools of thought:

- **The Holy Chemtrailers**, a numerically dominant faction whose contributions ranged from the succinct (*"Chemtrails"*) to the slightly more developed (*"Holy chemtrails"*), and who appeared to view each subsequent comment as a vital opportunity to confirm what the previous commenter had already said.
- **The Documentarians**, who reported that **Prime TV** is releasing a forthcoming movie about the dangers of the sky, and that **32 U.S. states have successfully banned this toxic spraying** — a statistic which could not be independently verified by this publication, the United States Environmental Protection Agency, or a map.
- **The Cloud Seeding Conflationists**, who patiently explained that what the original poster had photographed was "cloud seeding aka chemtrails," a definition which fuses two unrelated things into one larger and more frightening thing, in keeping with established community practice.
- **One Top Contributor** who replied simply: *"or…clouds."*

The **"or…clouds"** comment, posted by the original poster herself in apparent self-defense of her own photograph, received seven likes and was widely understood as a quiet act of civic heroism. It was followed by no further engagement from her on the thread, sources say, because by that point she had clearly accepted that the sky was no longer hers.

A separate commenter, citing personal observation, reported that her own view *that morning* had also contained the sky, and that this made her **angry** — a position that earned several reactions and a follow-up photograph of, sources confirm, **a different patch of sky**, included as evidence.

By the time the dust had settled, the original poster had **manually disabled commenting on the post**, joining a growing class of local residents who post a photograph of something nice, are immediately informed that the nice thing is in fact a coordinated chemical assault on the public, and then quietly turn off the comments and go to bed.

Reached for comment, **Environment and Climate Change Canada** referred this publication to a document titled *"Yes, Those Are Just Clouds, We Promise, We Have Looked Into It Several Times"* (revised edition). The document explains, in patient prose, that **contrails** — short for *condensation trails* — are produced when hot, humid jet engine exhaust meets cold upper-atmosphere air and condenses into ice crystals, in much the same way one's breath becomes briefly visible on a January morning, and which is **not, sources stress, a state secret.**

**Cloud seeding**, the document goes on to note, is a real and limited weather-modification technique used in a small number of jurisdictions to encourage precipitation, typically involving silver iodide dispersed at altitude under specific atmospheric conditions, and has **never been confirmed to have been deployed over a casino parking lot in Port Perry** at any point in the historical record.

The document closes by noting that the sky, while admittedly often quite striking, is — on balance — usually just the sky.

A passing dog, asked whether it had observed any unusual activity overhead, looked up briefly, sneezed, and continued walking.

At time of publication, one local resident had reportedly begun drafting a follow-up post asking whether the **rainbow** seen above the fairgrounds on Saturday was also part of "the spraying," and another was preparing a comment, on a separate and entirely unrelated post about a sunset, reading simply: *"Holy chemtrails."*
