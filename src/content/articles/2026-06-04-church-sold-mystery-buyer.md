Residents of a Durham Region hamlet are demanding answers regarding the recent sale of a local church, *The Corn* has learned, on the grounds that the private real estate transaction of two parties unknown to them is, in their view, a matter of urgent public concern.

The church — a modest brick building of indeterminate denomination, located beside a building of indeterminate function — was reportedly sold at some point in the recent past, according to a community member who noticed a sign and then noticed the sign was gone. Pressed for further details, the community member had none, and posted to ask if anyone else did.

Within hours, the post had attracted dozens of comments from residents who also did not know any further details but who agreed strongly that someone, somewhere, should be made to disclose them.

"I have no idea why this is a big secret," wrote one local woman, of a transaction between two private parties that neither of them had any obligation to announce to her. "But apparently it is."

A second resident clarified that "only the good Lord knows" who had purchased the property, a statement that was not factually accurate — the buyer is, presumably, also aware — but which nevertheless attracted four likes and a thumbs-up.

A longtime resident expressed hope that "whoever bought it fixes it up and restores it to the beautiful building it was," establishing in advance the standard against which the new owner's renovation choices, currently unknown, will be judged. The comment did not specify what beauty was being referenced, when the building had last possessed it, or whether the resident had been inside the building in the past decade.

*The Corn* spoke with a township staff member who confirmed that the sale of a private property is a private matter, that the township does not, as a general rule, issue press releases about who has purchased which building, and that anyone with a sincere interest in the chain of title for a parcel of land can obtain that information from the land registry for a fee of approximately thirty dollars.

"It's all public," she said. "It's been public the whole time."

Asked whether any resident in the comment thread had paid the thirty dollars, she said she did not know but suspected not, on the grounds that the price of mild ongoing speculation is zero and the price of an answer is thirty.

By Wednesday evening, the rumour had refined itself into several competing theories, including that the church had been purchased by a developer (to be torn down), by a private family (to be lived in), by a yoga studio (to be downward-dogged in), by a cult (to be cult-in), and by an unnamed wealthy outsider whose plans were variously catastrophic and unspecified. None of the theories were supported by evidence. All of them were supported by likes.

A separate thread proposed that the township should "do something," though it was not clear what, since the transaction had been conducted between two willing private parties under existing Ontario property law, with which the township is broadly compliant.

One resident posted simply: "SAD."

The comment, which referred to a transaction whose details, parties, timing, sale price, and future use were all unknown to the commenter, received nine likes.

At press time, the church remained sold. The buyer remained the buyer. The seller remained the seller. The land registry remained open for business. No member of the community had availed themselves of it. The comment thread continued to grow.

The rumour will be considered fully resolved as soon as someone tells everyone.
