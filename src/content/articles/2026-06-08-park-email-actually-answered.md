A longtime resident this week sent a politely-worded email to the Township regarding ongoing concerns at a local off-leash park and **received an actual reply containing actionable information**, sources confirmed Tuesday.

The resident, who asked not to be named because she is "not normally one to make a fuss," reported speaking directly with her ward councillor after her initial email "crashed" — a technical issue she described with the calm resignation of a woman who has been emailing the Township since 2014.

The councillor reportedly confirmed a series of concrete outcomes:

- The park will move to a **bi-weekly maintenance schedule**, a sentence that has not been uttered in this municipality since Confederation.
- A **weather-worn sign** that has been universally agreed to be an eyesore since approximately 2019 has been removed, with no committee struck, no public consultation held, and no commemorative plaque proposed.
- The **mud hole** — a beloved geographical feature that has variously been described as "a hazard," "character," and "where Buster goes" — will be filled.
- Staff are **considering removing the hydrant**, a development that has already split the community.

"Keep the hydrant," wrote one local man in the comments, beginning what regional historians believe will become a foundational text of the Save The Hydrant movement. By press time, three additional residents had proposed organizing informal classes around the hydrant, a community garden adjacent to the hydrant, and a small annual festival in the hydrant's honour.

The hydrant could not be reached for comment.

In a less uplifting development, funding for a proposed **shelter** has been withdrawn, meaning park-goers will continue to experience weather the way God intended: directly, and with complaints. The woodchip bin, however, will be "regularly monitored and filled up as needed," a phrase that residents have already begun cross-stitching onto throw pillows.

Reached for comment outside the park Wednesday morning, one regular visitor — accompanied by a goldendoodle named Pepper — said the developments represented "what small town democracy is supposed to look like."

"She emailed them," the visitor said, gesturing vaguely in the direction of the parking lot. "And then they emailed her back. And then *things happened*. In our **lifetimes**."

A passing terrier, asked for comment, sniffed the hydrant approvingly.
