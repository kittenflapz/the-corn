The township has reportedly selected a national convenience-store chain to serve as the anchor tenant of a new downtown mega mall, according to a source who heard the news from a friend, who heard it from a different friend, who has not been located for comment, *The Corn* has learned.

The development — described variously as a "mega mall," a "plaza," a "new shopping centre like in the city," and "you know, the thing they're putting in" — has not been formally proposed at council, does not appear on any agenda from the past eighteen months, has no application on file with the planning department, and is not depicted on any publicly available concept rendering. A vacant downtown lot where demolition is currently underway has been identified by multiple residents as "obviously" the future site, on the grounds that something must be going there.

"They wouldn't tear it down if they weren't building something massive," one local woman explained, gesturing at the lot, which is approximately the size of two-and-a-half parking spaces. "Mark my words. Mega mall."

The convenience-store chain — selected, according to the rumour, on the basis of unspecified criteria by an unspecified body within the township — was reached for comment by *The Corn* on the first try, by telephone, and was unable to confirm any details of the arrangement, including its existence.

"We don't have any record of that," a spokesperson said. "I don't know where that came from."

Asked whether the chain was in negotiations to anchor a yet-unannounced commercial development in a small Durham Region community, the spokesperson said no.

Asked whether the chain had ever, in its corporate history, served as the anchor tenant of anything, the spokesperson paused.

"We're a convenience store," she said.

The township, similarly reached for comment, similarly had no idea what anyone was talking about. A staff member confirmed that the lot in question is privately owned, that the demolition is a private matter between the owner and a private contractor, and that the municipality has issued no permits beyond the standard ones required to take down a building safely. Asked whether the township had recently selected an anchor tenant for any commercial development, the staff member said that the township does not select anchor tenants for any commercial development under any circumstances, ever.

"That's not a thing we do," she said. "That's not a thing that exists."

The clarifications did not appear to land. By Tuesday afternoon, the rumour had been further refined in community discussion to include a food court (three units, one of them a sushi place), a movie theatre (multiplex, six screens, IMAX optional), and an underground parking garage (heated). One resident reported that her sister-in-law had seen the blueprints. Pressed on where, she said her sister-in-law had not actually seen the blueprints but had been told about them by a person who had.

A second resident, asked how she had come to know about the mega mall, said she had heard about it from a friend.

The friend, asked the same question, said she had heard about it from a friend.

At press time, the lot remained a lot. A single excavator continued to push debris into a tidier pile of debris. The convenience-store chain continued to be a convenience-store chain. No mall, mega or otherwise, has been proposed, approved, designed, funded, or, in any meaningful sense, imagined by anyone in a position to imagine it.

The rumour continues to spread.
