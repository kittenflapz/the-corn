A longtime resident this week posted to a local community group demanding to know whether the Mayor was "doing anything" for the Town in connection with the upcoming World Cup, suggesting that the absence of municipally-organized watch parties represented a unique civic failure on the part of this community alone, sources confirmed Wednesday afternoon.

The post — which has since generated **ten comments and five likes**, a level of engagement local political scientists describe as "above replacement" — argued that the Town may in fact be the **only municipality in Canada** whose Mayor does not personally consider hosting the World Cup to be "that important," a claim that has not been independently verified by Elections Canada, Statistics Canada, or anyone with a working knowledge of how mayors spend their afternoons.

Within minutes, the comment section had splintered into three warring factions:

- **The Jurisdictional Realists**, who patiently noted that watch parties are generally organized by "the soccer association or maybe even the bars," and that organizing such events is "not really the duty of the mayor." This camp was rewarded with a small but loyal following of four likes and the quiet respect of anyone who has ever read a municipal bylaw.
- **The Constructive Cynics**, who suggested that "hours of organizing would be better spent" by literally anyone other than an elected official — a position widely understood as polite Ontarian for *have you considered doing it yourself.*
- **One man** who replied simply, *"What's the World Cup?"* This comment received four likes and was immediately followed by a top contributor clarifying that the World Cup is **"real life fooseball,"** a definition that has not been formally challenged and may now stand as the community's official position.

A separate commenter weighed in to note that the Mayor "does a great job" and that perhaps the original poster should consider **setting up a watch party themselves** — a suggestion that, according to local custom, must be met with a thirty-six-hour cooling-off period before the original poster either (a) ignores it entirely or (b) responds that they "shouldn't have to."

By press time, no watch parties had been scheduled. No bars had posted. The soccer association had not weighed in. The Mayor's office, reached for comment, was reportedly doing the actual job of being a mayor, which involves a number of things, most of which are not soccer.

A passing dog, asked whether the Town was failing in its responsibilities to host the World Cup, sniffed at a flyer for an unrelated bake sale and moved on.

At time of publication, one resident had reportedly begun typing a follow-up post asking whether the Mayor planned to do anything about the **Olympics**, the **King's Birthday**, or **the weather**.
